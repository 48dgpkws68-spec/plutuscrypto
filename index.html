The name

Plutus was blinded so that
wealth would find everyone

In Greek myth Plutus carried the wealth of the world. Zeus took his sight so that he could no longer tell the deserving from the undeserving, and would hand out fortune without prejudice.

That is a strange wish to make of a god, and a very sensible thing to ask of a payment network. Ours does not care which chain your customer holds, which wallet they opened this morning, or which country issued their passport. It takes the coin, and it pays the merchant.

How a payment travels

From wallet to bank account

Your customer picks an asset

They see your name and your colours, choose the coin and network they hold, and get an address plus a QR code. Nothing is installed and no account is needed on their side.

The rate is locked for fifteen minutes

We quote an exact amount of crypto against your invoice in euros, pounds or dollars, and hold it. If the market moves inside that window, it moves against us and not against you.

The network confirms

We watch the transaction from the moment it hits the mempool. You can release goods on first sight for small tickets, or wait for the confirmation depth you set per asset.

You are settled in real money

Converted at the locked rate and paid out to your bank account the next business day, or kept in stablecoin if you would rather hold a float for payouts and refunds.
